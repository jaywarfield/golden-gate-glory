# **Mission Veteran AI** *by G3*

#### Problem and Solution

Military career transition is a personal productivity crisis at national scale. Every year, 200,000 U.S. service members transition to civilian life — and the federal government's own hiring systems cannot read their résumés. Military job titles, rank structure, and MOS/AFSC codes are invisible to the ATS systems used by federal agencies and government contractors. A veteran with 8 years managing enterprise networks and $2M in infrastructure registers as zero matches for "Network Administrator" — not because the experience is absent, but because the language does not translate. One in three veterans is under-employed in their first civilian role (Bureau of Labor Statistics). What previously took weeks of manual research — crosswalking a military specialty code to civilian skills, finding the right job description, identifying skill gaps, sourcing free training, and building an evidence-locked résumé — can now be completed in a single session.
  
Mission Veteran AI (by Golden Gate Glory, G3) directly addresses this double productivity loss — veterans waste weeks on a résumé that ATS systems reject on first pass, while federal agencies spend months searching for qualified candidates hidden behind military jargon. It is a free, AI-powered career transition tool powered by IBM Granite 4 on watsonx.ai as its core AI engine, coordinated by watsonx Orchestrate, and built with IBM Bob as development assistant — targeting veterans pursuing federal government, defense contractor, and public-sector IT roles. Mission Veteran AI eliminates both losses in a single session: veterans describe their service; IBM Granite 4 translates it into a government-ready civilian résumé grounded in O*NET crosswalks, targeted to a specific job description, and exported only after a human sign-off gate resolves every unverified claim.

IBM Bob delivered measurable team productivity gains: the entire project — seven agents, twelve RAG knowledge bases, Jupyter notebooks, Terraform infrastructure scripts, a FastAPI backend, and a seven-screen web UI — was designed, implemented, debugged, and documented by a three-person team using IBM Bob as the primary development assistant throughout. IBM Bob drafted code, caught logic errors, rewrote prompts, and produced this submission. Without IBM Bob, this scope of work would not have been achievable within the competition window — it multiplied what a small team could deliver. For the veterans the solution serves, the productivity gain is equally concrete: O*NET crosswalk maps the MOS/AFSC code to a civilian skill inventory in seconds; gap analysis against the job description's required skills identifies exactly what is missing and recommends free IBM SkillsBuild courses and DoD SkillBridge programs to close those gaps; and every résumé sentence is tagged EVIDENCED, INFERRED, or MISSING so the veteran knows precisely what to verify before the document is released — compressing weeks of manual research into a single session.
